# Capture expert judgement where work already happens

The best and most feasible way for Keystone to elicit tacit operational know-how is not a single modality. It is a four-stage pipeline. **Mine the existing records first, then run short, incident-anchored AI interviews with a few critical experts, then gather frontline evidence through event-triggered voice micro-capture, and confirm everything as atomic, quote-grounded rule tiles.** Questionnaires belong only at the confirmation stage. The evidence for this is strong on one point. Unaided self-report hits a hard ceiling: no single expert verbalised more than **29–53%** of their own actions, whatever elicitation method was used, and pooling six experts raised coverage to **62–88%** ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf)). AI interviewers now match human interviewers on transcript quality and satisfaction, but every one of those studies measured opinions, not procedures. LLMs also recover **fewer than half of implicit requirements** and under-produce exactly the conditions and exceptions that decision rules depend on ([ReqElicitGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18306v1)). Keystone's existing `/capture` design (CDM/ACTA probes, gap-targeting, contradiction catch, provenance quotes, Canvas confirmation, deterministic gates) is therefore the right core and should be kept. What it lacks is a frontline channel, real voice with Singapore-capable speech recognition, multi-expert pooling and defences against rubber-stamping. In Singapore, three constraints matter: stock Whisper error rates of roughly **30–47% on conversational Singapore English**, PDPA notice and retention duties for recorded voice, and older experts' job-insecurity-driven knowledge hiding. These shape the build more than model choice does. The recommended hackathon cut is about **18 hours**: override-triggered capture tasks, hold-to-talk close-out voice notes that land as draft tiles, numeric read-back, a peer-confirm badge and a QR "tap to teach" entry point.

## Experts can articulate only a third to half of what they do

The central problem is not finding the right interface. It is that expert knowledge is automated and largely unavailable to introspection, so "experts' unaided self-reports of their problem-solving processes are typically inaccurate or incomplete" ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf)). The measured gaps are large and consistent. In Chao and Salvendy's troubleshooting study, no programmer reported more than **41% of diagnostic actions, 53% of debugging actions or 29% of interpretations**, whatever the elicitation method. Coverage rose to 87%, 88% and 62% only when six experts were pooled, because each expert had automated different sub-skills ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf)). Expert colorectal surgeons described only **26–50% of procedural steps and 25–43% of decision points** that cognitive task analysis later identified from video ([Sullivan et al.](https://faculty.med.virginia.edu/facultyaffairs/files/2016/04/2015-6-9b-1.pdf)). In the neonatal ICU, CTA probes surfaced **25 of 70 cues (more than a third)** that were absent from the training literature, spanning seven unrecognised categories ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf)). The often-quoted "experts omit 70% of decisions" is a review-level synthesis from Clark's group, not a single measurement. Keystone should present the defensible version: self-report captures roughly a third to a half of what experts actually do.

This finding settles the questionnaire question. A blank form ("describe how you handle a chiller trip") is unaided self-report in its purest form. The literature predicts it will return the SOP plus rationalisations. One physician admitted teaching a reconstructed "plausible" method because "I don't know how I do diagnosis" ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf)). A team of engineers who made "extensive and repeated efforts" to write assembly instructions still omitted a universal step, and the equipment did not work (same source). Probing is what adds tacit content. Incident-anchored methods reach cues, anomalies and exceptions. The Critical Decision Method (CDM) applies multi-pass probes to real non-routine events. ACTA's knowledge audit and simulation interview took "up to two hours" and "often elicits previously undocumented aspects" ([BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/psychologist/methods-accessing-expert-cognition)). Structured interviews outperform think-aloud, sorting and ranking in efficiency ([Burton et al. 1990](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/the-efficacy-of-knowledge-elicitation-techniques-a-comparison-acr/); [Dieste & Juristo review](https://ebse.webspace.durham.ac.uk/ebse-bibliography/effectiveness-of-requirements-elicitation-techniques-empirical-results-derived-from-a-systematic-review/)). Hoffman judged structured interviews the only method whose output arrives "in a format ready to be input into the data base" ([Hoffman 1987](https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/583)). No controlled study compares written questionnaires with interviews for tacit operational knowledge. That gap is real. Even so, the indirect evidence all points one way: forms work as recognition tasks over candidate rules ("is this right, when does it not apply, rank these cues"), not as authoring tools.

Two further classic findings matter for speed. First, the expert's time is not the dominant cost; the analyst's is. In Hoffman's airlift study, 10 hours of unstructured interviews consumed **100 hours of total task time**, about **0.13 new propositions per task-minute**, against his "on the right track" benchmark of about 2 ([Hoffman 1987](https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/583)). LLM transcription and extraction attack exactly this 10:1 analyst overhead. Second, yield falls sharply within a session, from more than 200 propositions per hour early on to about 40 later (same source). That argues for short, focused sessions rather than long "tell me everything" interviews. Validation also has a clear template. Interview at least two or three experts separately "to avoid premature consensus", and treat disagreement as informative ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf); [Hoffman 1987](https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/583)). Coded CDM data shows experts agree on cues (κ ≈ .81) far more than on actions (κ = .50) ([Militello & Lim 1995](https://www.shadowboxtraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/A-Cognitive-Task-Analysis-of-NICU-Nurses-Patient-Assessment-Skills-by-Miletello1995.pdf)). Keystone's action rules will therefore need more conditionality than its triggers. The payoff is well documented. CTA-based training shows Hedges' **g = 0.87** across studies ([Tofel-Grehl & Feldon 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1555343412474821)). In the closest analogue to facility troubleshooting, Navy radar students trained on CTA-derived content "solved more than twice as many malfunctions, in less time" ([Clark et al. 2008](https://hpttreasures.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cta_chapter_2008.pdf)).

## AI interviewers match humans on rapport but miss the rule's conditions

Acceptance of an AI interviewer is not the risk. Chopra and Haaland's 381 AI-led interviews found that most respondents strictly preferred AI to human interviewers ([CESifo](https://cesifo.org/en/publications/2023/working-paper/conducting-qualitative-interviews-ai)). Geiecke and Jaravel's open-source single-agent interviewer was validated against human experts with "high performance ratings" ([CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/dp19705)). A randomised student study found AI data quality "comparable to traditional methods" ([Wuttke et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01824)). A 40-person pilot showed no significant difference in response length, specificity or relevance between a human interviewer and a 7B model ([AInterviewer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20588)). Anthropic's 1,250 adaptive 10–15 minute interviews drew **97.6%** satisfaction of 5 or higher on a 7-point scale, and **96.96%** said the interview captured their thoughts ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-interviewer)). The same study reported a warning Keystone should act on: self-reported behaviour (65% augmentative use) diverged from observed usage data (47%). What an engineer says in an interview will differ from what the work orders show, which is why behaviour-anchored validation against records belongs in the loop.

The weakness is in content, not rapport. Every study above elicited attitudes or opinions. None tested procedural or tacit operational knowledge. Where structure was measured, LLMs fell short on the parts that matter for decision rules. Across seven models and 101 scenarios, LLM interviewers elicited **fewer than half of users' implicit requirements**, and their useful questions arrived late in the dialogue ([ReqElicitGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18306v1)). LLM-built process models captured content and actors well but contained **fewer activities, events and gateways** than human models ([Utrecht thesis](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/48957)). Gateways are where triggers, exceptions and thresholds live. Structure helps. An ontology-guided interviewer improved early discovery of implicit requirements by about **21%** relative to the best free-chat baseline ([OntoAgent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05828)). LLMREI made "a similar number of errors" to human interviewers ([Korn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02564)). And language-model elicitation through generated edge cases was "often more informative than user-written prompts", with less reported mental effort ([GATE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11589)). This validates Keystone's design choice: the rule schema (trigger, required evidence, exceptions, forbidden actions, escalation) works as the ontology, and an empty slot is the gap the interviewer must target next.

"Confirm, don't author" is faster, but it has a documented failure mode. Ophthalmologists editing LLM drafts often left answers uncorrected if they were "not completely wrong", even when the drafts diverged from their own practice ([Editing with AI, NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19940)). Span-anchored AI suggestions produced more targeted revisions and a stronger sense of ownership than chat, at higher effort ([AnchoredAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16128v1)). Keystone's per-tile confirm/edit/reject with a provenance quote is the anchored pattern. It still needs a forcing function against rubber-stamping, such as one scenario probe per tile ("what would make this rule wrong?") and occasional seeded canary tiles. On quality control, intrinsic self-refinement did not significantly improve LLM extraction and raised costs ([Brinkmann & Bizer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01237)). External checks do work. Requiring a verbatim quote that is mechanically matched to the source is the most defensible hallucination guard ([grounding-quote pattern](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04663)). GPT-4o traced extracted goals to source text with **98.7%** accuracy while matching only **62%** of human-extracted goals ([Generative Goal Modeling](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01048v1)). The same study's practice of sampling the extraction several times and clustering the results is a cheap way to expose unstable clauses. Keystone should spend its budget on quote verification, multi-sample disagreement and replay against work orders, not on a self-critique loop.

## Voice carries richer answers, but Singapore speech defeats stock ASR

A randomised comparison found that spoken answers to open questions were longer and scored higher on informativeness and personal experience than typed answers ([Galasso, Nannicini & Nozza](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp17488)). For busy engineers, voice is the right default. Keystone today has no microphone path at all: the expert side of `/capture` is scripted and read out by browser text-to-speech (per the project README). Real voice is the single largest gap between the demo and a deployable product.

Speech recognition is the binding constraint. Whisper's error rate on Singapore English is nearly **twice** its US English rate on identical text ([arXiv 2506.16969](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16969)). Whisper-large-v3 reaches about **47.5% WER** on MNSC Part 4 conversational speech, against about 7% on read speech ([arXiv 2501.01034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01034)). Localisation largely fixes this. MERaLiON-AudioLLM, trained on Singapore-accented and code-switched data, reports about **5% WER** on Singapore-focused tests against about 33% for stock Whisper-large-v2, though these are developer-run evaluations ([MERaLiON model card](https://huggingface.co/MERaLiON/MERaLiON-AudioLLM-Whisper-SEA-LION)). A Whisper-turbo fine-tuned on IMDA NSC Part 3 data fell from 27.58% to **13.35%** on a Singlish benchmark (self-reported) ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/mjwong/whisper-large-v3-turbo-singlish)). Code-switching clusters around nouns ([BITS Pilani/NTU](https://www.bits-pilani.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/Singaporean_Conversational_English-Malay_Code-Switching_Speech_An_Analysis_Based_on_Code-switching_Points_and_Part_-of-Speech.pdf)), and nouns (equipment tags, valve IDs) are what matter in a rule. Even with a good model, a misheard setpoint or deadline becomes a wrong gate. Keystone therefore needs a domain glossary for the asset register, and a mandatory read-back that shows every number, asset tag and role as a tap-to-confirm chip before a tile can leave Draft. No WER benchmark exists for noisy Singapore plant rooms, and none exists for Tamil, Bengali or Burmese technical speech. Capture should happen after the job, in a quieter space, with hold-to-talk rather than ambient recording.

Latency shapes the architecture. No primary source sets an acceptable turn-taking latency for voice interviewers. Responsiveness is linked to completion and satisfaction only qualitatively ([arXiv 2507.17718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17718)). Keystone's own measured extraction latency on TokenHub/hy4-preview was **p50 11.4 s, p95 12.9 s** (10 of 10 live calls, per the README). That is fine for asynchronous drafting but far too slow for full-duplex conversation. The fast design for both the expert and the system is turn-based push-to-talk. The expert speaks a 15–60 second answer, ASR returns a transcript the expert can glance-correct, the interviewer's next probe is chosen from a precomputed gap list (no LLM round trip), and rule extraction runs in the background while the conversation continues. This keeps the expert's wait time near zero without needing a sub-second voice stack.

## Scaled field-capture systems bind capture to events, not blank forms

The open-source projects that reached millions of frontline workers share one design. They never ask workers to write knowledge. ODK and KoboToolbox are used by more than 14,000 organisations, with 20M+ surveys a month (self-reported) ([KoboToolbox](https://kobotoolbox.org/about-us/software)). CommCare reports more than 1M frontline workers ([Dimagi](https://dimagi.com/press-kit/documents/dimagi-boilerplate.txt)). The Community Health Toolkit serves about 200k health workers and is national infrastructure in seven governments ([DPGA](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/blog/dpg-spotlight-2025-community-health-toolkit)). RapidPro's U-Report reached about 13–14M users ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-Report)). These systems attach capture to an existing case or event, **preload context so the worker answers only the delta** ([ODK Entities](https://docs.getodk.org/entities-intro/)), never block on connectivity or structure, and push structuring and verification to a different person with a visible status. Ushahidi made every inbound message a draft post for human triage. In Haiti in 2010, about 40k reports became about 4k structured events ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushahidi)). iNaturalist upgrades a machine-suggested identification to "research grade" only after peers confirm it ([repo](https://github.com/inaturalist/iNaturalistAndroid)). That maps directly onto Keystone's Draft→Shadow→Approved states.

The newest projects now insert ASR plus an LLM between raw voice and structured fields while keeping human review. KoboToolbox added AI analysis of audio answers in 2026 and stores each AI value with its engine, revisions and verification status, marking AI versus manual values in exports ([Kobo forum](https://community.kobotoolbox.org/t/may-2026-feature-update/76236); [Kobo blog](https://kobotoolbox.org/blog/improving-qualitative-data-collection-kobotoolboxs-advanced-features-for-transcription-translation-and-analysis)). Glific, used by about 100 Indian NGOs, runs WhatsApp voice notes through speech-to-text and then an LLM "parse into these fields" step inside a chat flow ([Glific](https://github.com/glific/glific); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/socialstory/2021/08/glific-open-source-platform-ngo-whatsapp-chatbots/amp)). Atlas CMMS puts a voice recorder directly on work orders ([Atlas CMMS](https://github.com/Grashjs/cmms)). The cautionary case is Gram Vaani's Mobile Vaani voice IVR. It claims about 2M users, but its own write-up says **"1000+ hours of audio … manually transcribed, tagged and curated"** slowed the feedback loop and limited scale ([MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/7355)). That human-curation bottleneck is precisely what LLM extraction plus expert confirmation removes. None of these projects turns field input into a versioned, deterministic decision rule, which leaves room for Keystone's differentiation. Most of them (Glific, RapidPro, CHT, Kobo, Atlas) are AGPL-licensed and should serve as design references, not embedded code.

Commercial practice confirms the gap from the other side. Connected-worker platforms (Poka, Dozuki, Augmentir) codify procedural knowledge through filmed or AI-drafted work instructions. CMMS copilots such as MaintainX CoPilot mine past work orders and manuals into suggestions that always require human approval ([MaintainX](https://help.getmaintainx.com/complete-a-work-order-with-maintainx-copilot); [ReliaMag](https://reliamag.com/guides/best-connected-worker-platforms-2026/)). FM majors (JLL Falcon, CBRE Ellis) sit in the retrieval and prediction camp ([Facilities Dive](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/jll-serve-cbre-cushman-wakefield-artificial-intelligence-ai-initiatives-integrated-FM/719271/)). None of these products publicly offers structured elicitation of an expert's diagnostic judgement, and none has independently verified outcome numbers. Work-order mining is a cheap first pass, but it can recover only what technicians actually wrote. No study measures its yield against interviews, so Keystone should treat mined rules as hypotheses to confirm, not as rules.

Two field findings shape how micro-capture should work. On timing, eyewitness-memory research recommends initial recall **within 24 hours**, with benefits lasting at least a month ([PMC9225701](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9225701/)). One randomised police study found timing made no difference ([J Exp Criminol](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s11292-018-9348-0)), so the case for same-shift capture is supportive rather than decisive. Hoffman's "method of tough cases" already had experts tape-record hard cases as they happened ([Hoffman 1987](https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/583)). On adoption, frontline tools fail when they add friction. **75% of leaders believe they invest in frontline tech, but only 39% of frontline workers agree** ([Axonify via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/new-research-reveals-critical-gaps-in-mobile-technology-for-frontline-22218657)), and companies expect workers to juggle six or more enterprise apps a day ([Point B](https://www.pointb.com/Insights/Articles/2024/07/Equipping-Your-Deskless-Workforce-for-the-Future)). Deloitte's failure list adds that "daily documentation demands are rarely effective" ([Deloitte 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/knowledge-management-plan.html)). Capture must ride on a moment the worker already has to close out (a work order, an override, an incident), and it should take seconds.

Retention programmes show what throughput is realistic. Delta triaged 120 critical maintenance staff by departure date and uniqueness, and interviewed **85 of them in under a month** ([DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf)). TVA ranked people by an attrition factor (retirement timing × position risk) (same source). Those same programmes found that **reuse, not capture**, was the unresolved problem: transcripts needed heavy editing, and nobody knew whether the knowledge bases were accessed. Keystone's route from rule to gate to incident decision answers that failure mode directly. Uptime Institute found that faulty procedures account for **40%** of human-error data-centre outages ([Uptime](https://network.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/outages-understanding-human-factor)), which supports capturing corrections to procedures, not just more procedures.

## Trust, PDPA and outsourcing decide feasibility in Singapore FM

The demographic window is defined and closing. About **40% of resident built-environment PMETs are 50 or older** ([MND 2022](https://mnd.gov.sg/newsroom/speeches/view/speech-by-sms-tan-at-the-gala-dinner-for-world-workplace-asia-pacific-2022)), and BCA describes FM as manpower-intensive with an ageing workforce ([BCA](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/resources/newsroom/speech-by-mos-zaqy-mohamad-at-the-6th-international-facility-management-conference/)). Retirement and re-employment ages rose to **64 and 69 on 1 July 2026** ([Singapore Law Watch](https://www.singaporelawwatch.sg/Headlines/singapore-to-raise-retirement-age-to-64-and-re-employment-age-to-69-on-july-1-tan-see-leng)), which gives Keppel a known horizon for each senior engineer. Much FM labour is contracted out (64% in 2017 data, [Statista](https://www-statista-com.ezproxy.canberra.edu.au/statistics/1020504/singapore-breakdown-fm-workforce-by-employment-type)). The knowledge therefore often sits with a contractor's staff, and contract terms on who owns captured rules matter as much as tooling.

Trust is the human constraint. A time-lagged study of 219 older workers found that age-stereotype threat raises job insecurity, which drives knowledge hiding, and that a strong supervisor relationship mitigates it ([Duan et al. 2025](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JKM-03-2024-0324/full/html)). Workers who feel their careers have plateaued hide tacit knowledge more than explicit knowledge ([MPU](https://research.mpu.edu.mo/en/publications/exploring-factors-triggering-individual-knowledge-hiding-at-the-e/)). In practice, capture should be led by a trusted chief engineer rather than HQ, run in paid time, carry opt-in named attribution ("rule by CE Tan"), and come with a written commitment that it will never be used for appraisal. The refinery manager in DeLong's study put it this way: "I had always assumed people were withholding knowledge, but in reality they just didn't have a forum to share it" ([DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf)).

PDPA is manageable but not automatic. Employers may collect employee personal data without consent for purposes reasonably related to managing employment, but must notify employees of the purpose under s.20(4)–(5). Any other purpose relying on legitimate interests needs a documented assessment, kept on file and disclosed in a public policy ([PDPC Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts, rev. Apr 2026](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-key-concepts/advisory-guidelines-on-key-concepts-in-the-pdpa-17-may-2022.pdf)). Voice recordings, transcripts and attributed quotes are personal data. They are subject to access requests and to the retention limitation obligation in s.25 (same source). Whether building a corporate knowledge base counts as "managing the employment relationship" is untested, so the safe pattern is explicit notice plus consent for recording and attribution. Keystone should keep the expert-approved transcript span as provenance and expire raw audio on a schedule. An external ASR/LLM vendor is likely a data intermediary needing contractual safeguards. Offshore processing raises transfer questions that this research did not resolve. Note that Keystone's TokenHub endpoint is a non-Singapore region (README). Ambient capture through glasses, body-cams or open mics records co-workers and tenants and looks like monitoring. Explicit push-to-record is far lower risk.

## Recommendation: a four-stage capture flow for Keystone

**Keep** the AI onboarding interview as the primary instrument for the few critical experts. That means the CDM/ACTA probes, gap-targeting from empty schema slots, contradiction catch resolved by a distinguishing condition, quote-grounded tiles, Canvas confirm/edit/reject, deterministic gates, governed versions and eval replay. These implement the best-evidenced parts of the literature. **Change** the interview's shape. Split it into two or three 20–30 minute push-to-talk sessions, each anchored on one CDM incident triplet (a call that went right, one that went wrong, one that was nearly missed) ([Militello & Lim 1995](https://www.shadowboxtraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/A-Cognitive-Task-Analysis-of-NICU-Nurses-Patient-Assessment-Skills-by-Miletello1995.pdf)). Seed each session with draft rules mined from that expert's work orders, so probes target gaps rather than re-asking documented procedure. Interview at least three engineers per critical system separately, and auto-flag conflicts across experts as "conditional rule or adjudicate". **Build** the frontline channel: override-triggered and close-out voice micro-capture, numeric read-back and peer confirmation. **Do not build** questionnaire-first authoring, ambient or smart-glasses capture, a live IVR, a full-duplex real-time voice stack or a self-critique loop. The evidence does not support them, or they cost more than they return.

| Method | Verdict for Keystone | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AI-led structured interview (CDM/ACTA) | **Core, keep** for critical experts | Matches humans on quality and acceptance; schema-driven probing counters implicit-requirement misses |
| Human-led structured interview | **Fallback and calibration arm** | Gold standard, but about 10 analyst-hours per interview-hour; use for pilot comparison and very senior or reluctant experts |
| Questionnaire or form | **Confirmation only** | Unaided self-report ceiling; forms work as recognition tasks over drafted rules |
| Voice notes at close-out | **Build** | Richer than text; rides an existing step; needs Singapore ASR plus read-back |
| Incident/override-triggered micro-capture | **Build, highest value** | Same-shift recall; every gate override becomes a rule-learning event |
| Work-order mining | **Build as pre-seed** | Zero expert time; yield capped by note quality; treat output as hypotheses |
| Scenario confirmation ("what makes this wrong?") | **Build** | Edge-case elicitation beats self-specification; counters rubber-stamping |
| Chat/WhatsApp capture | **Later; Telegram for demo** | Low friction, but personal-account governance under PDPA; production needs WhatsApp Business API or CMMS-native |
| Peer confirmation | **Build** | Pooling raises coverage to 62–88%; the research-grade pattern adds governance credibility |
| IVR | **Simulate only** | Inclusion story for gloved or no-smartphone staff; high live risk, low hackathon return |

The time estimates below are design targets derived from the evidence, not measured Keystone results, and the pilot must validate them. Per critical expert, expect about **60–90 minutes of interview** across two or three sessions, against CDM's 1.5–2 hours per expert ([Militello & Lim 1995](https://www.shadowboxtraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/A-Cognitive-Task-Analysis-of-NICU-Nurses-Patient-Assessment-Skills-by-Miletello1995.pdf)). Add about **20–30 minutes of Canvas confirmation**, yielding roughly 10–15 candidate rules per system (the demo's 13 tiles are a plausible scale). That puts expert time per governed rule at about **6–9 minutes**. Steward/analyst time should fall from Hoffman's 10:1 ratio to about 1:1, because transcription, extraction and quote linking run automatically. For each frontline micro-capture, the worker spends **under 1 minute** (a 15–30 second voice note plus one confirm tap). The system needs about 15–25 seconds asynchronously: ASR, plus extraction at Keystone's measured p50 of 11.4 seconds, plus quote verification. Each rule upgrade from frontline evidence costs the chief engineer about **1–2 minutes** on the Canvas queue.

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Singapore ASR garbles thresholds, tags, code-switched nouns | Singapore-tuned ASR (MERaLiON-class or NSC fine-tuned; verify MERaLiON licence), asset glossary biasing, mandatory read-back chips for numbers, roles and tags; store raw transcript and engine stamp Kobo-style |
| Rubber-stamping of AI drafts | Per-tile atomic confirm, one scenario probe per tile, seeded canary tiles, track edit/reject rate (a near-zero edit rate is a warning sign) |
| Implicit conditions missed | Schema slots as ontology, multi-sample extraction disagreement, ≥3 experts per system, replay against historical work orders |
| PDPA (recording, attribution, retention, offshore processing) | s.20 notice plus consent for recording and named attribution, documented legitimate-interests assessment if relied on, audio expiry with approved transcript kept, data-intermediary contract, Singapore-region processing for production |
| Knowledge hiding and trust | CE-led capture in paid time, opt-in attribution, written no-appraisal commitment, contractor knowledge-ownership clause |
| Frontline adoption | Capture only at existing close-out or override moments, under 60 seconds, no new app (QR to web page, later CMMS-native) |

The pilot should run for **six weeks on one system** (chilled water at one office building, with the data-centre profile as a second site). It should be a within-subject comparison. Three chief or senior engineers each give an AI-led session on one subsystem and a human-led ACTA session on a matched subsystem, while technicians use close-out and override micro-capture in parallel. Measure governed rules per expert-hour, slot completeness (especially exceptions and escalation, the "gateways"), share of clauses with a mechanically verified quote, cross-expert agreement, expert edit/reject rate and canary catch rate, ASR WER on 50 sampled clips with numeric-token error reported separately, micro-capture response rate and median seconds, and agreement between rules and six to twelve months of historical work-order outcomes. The success bar should be set in advance. A reasonable one is AI-led sessions within ±20% of human ACTA on exception slots at under half the analyst time, at least 50% micro-capture response on prompted overrides, and zero ungated numeric errors reaching Approved.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Keystone's pitch. The hard problem was never getting experts to talk to an AI. People accept AI interviewers readily. The hard problems are that any one expert can articulate only part of what they know, and that LLMs systematically drop the conditions that turn advice into a safe rule. Keystone's real moat is not the interviewer. It is the combination of a schema that makes missing conditions visible, provenance that makes every clause checkable, and deterministic gates whose overrides feed back into capture. That turns knowledge capture from a one-off retirement interview into a continuous by-product of operations. No commercial or open-source system surveyed does this today.

Two uncertainties remain. First, no study has yet measured AI-led versus human-led elicitation of procedural know-how, or the yield of work-order mining against interviews, so Keystone's pilot would itself generate new evidence. Second, Singapore plant-room speech and the PDPA status of knowledge-base purposes are both unbenchmarked and untested. Both should be settled with Keppel's data-protection officer and a 50-clip ASR test before any production rollout, and before claiming more than a hackathon demo.
