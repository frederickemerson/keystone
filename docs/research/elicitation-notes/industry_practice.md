# Industry Practice: Capturing Frontline / Expert Operational Know-How (2022–2026, plus earlier retention programs)

Research scope note: ~20 searches/fetches (October 2026). Vendor sites were mostly reached through search summaries and third-party comparisons, not full case-study PDFs. Every quantitative outcome below is labelled **[VENDOR]** (vendor or vendor-affiliated source), **[CONSULTANCY]** (consultancy client story, not independently audited), **[PRIMARY/INSTITUTIONAL]** (regulator, IAEA, research institute, survey body) or **[SECONDARY]** (press or comparison blog). I found **no independently verified (peer-reviewed or third-party audited) outcome numbers** for any connected-worker or AI knowledge-capture product.

## Q1. Connected-worker / frontline knowledge platforms: capture method, AI features (2024–2026), published outcomes

### Takeaway
Connected-worker platforms mostly capture know-how as a side effect of digitising procedures. Experts author or film step-by-step instructions (video, photos, forms), and since about 2024 generative AI turns video, documents or work-order history into draft procedures, or answers technicians' questions from that corpus. They codify **procedural** knowledge well. None of them runs structured elicitation of an expert's tacit diagnostic reasoning. Published outcomes are almost all vendor-claimed, with onboarding/training-time reductions (about 50–80%) as the headline metric.

### Cited Findings
**Capture methods by vendor**
- **Poka (now owned by IFS):** the capture model is video. Experienced workers film short lessons and troubleshooting clips, tagged to equipment and workstations. Reviewers call it the "YouTube of the factory floor". Its AI turns existing documents, videos and expert knowledge into steps. — [Guideflow comparison](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/visual-work-instruction-software); [ReliaMag independent comparison](https://reliamag.com/guides/best-connected-worker-platforms-2026/); [Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/best-dozuki-alternatives-manufacturing/) [SECONDARY; Fabrico is a competing vendor]
- **Dozuki:** authoring tools for text, photo and video work instructions. Its AI tool "CreatorPro" drafts instructions from narrated video and legacy documents. Reviewers warn the AI output is a draft that people must review and publish, so an approval workflow is still needed. — [Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/best-dozuki-alternatives-manufacturing/); [manual.to AI work-instruction comparison](https://manual.to/compare/ai-work-instruction-software/) [SECONDARY, vendor-affiliated]
- **SwipeGuide:** minimalist, mobile-first instructions. Its listed differentiator is "AI authoring + instant translation". I found no detail on expert video capture. — [Guideflow](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/visual-work-instruction-software) [SECONDARY]
- **Augmentir:** digital work instructions with video, plus AR-annotated remote expert help. Its AI/ML adapts guidance to each worker and identifies individual skill gaps. It won a 2024 Brandon Hall gold award for generative AI business impact. — [ReliaMag](https://reliamag.com/guides/best-connected-worker-platforms-2026/); [Augmentir blog (DE)](https://www.augmentir.com/de/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/14346) [VENDOR/SECONDARY]
- **Parsable:** digitised procedures with conditional logic and timestamped data capture. No video- or voice-first capture is described. **Acquired by CAI Software (an STG portfolio company) on 25 September 2024.** — [ReliaMag](https://reliamag.com/guides/best-connected-worker-platforms-2026/); [Newswire acquisition release](https://www.newswire.com/news/cai-software-llc-acquires-parsable-inc-a-leading-connected-worker-22429489)
- **Tulip:** a no-code app builder. Users build their own capture and workflow apps, connected to PLCs and sensors. ReliaMag rates its AI as "via integrations / limited". — [ReliaMag](https://reliamag.com/guides/best-connected-worker-platforms-2026/) [SECONDARY]
- **Librestream Onsight:** live video with AR annotation and session recordings, so remote-expert calls become a capture channel. "AI visual recognition" identifies components and suggests troubleshooting steps. — [ReliaMag](https://reliamag.com/guides/best-connected-worker-platforms-2026/) [SECONDARY]
- **MaintainX CoPilot (CMMS, Enterprise plan):** mines **past work orders** (work-order summaries) and uploaded asset manuals. Capgemini sells a voice-enabled agentic troubleshooting assistant on AWS Marketplace. Microsoft announced a "Frontline Agent" for Teams. — [Databricks session](https://www.databricks.com/dataaisummit/session/ai-agents-frontline-7-elevens-genai-maintenance-assistant); [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-pxc7tlhrsrzli); [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftteamsblog/frontline-agent-an-out-of-the-box-ai-assistant-for-every-frontline-teammate/4492837)

**Analyst context**
- LNS Research (via a QAD Redzone blog, March 2026): nearly 3 in 4 manufacturers have seen operational performance decline because of difficulty hiring, training and retaining frontline workers, and over half of industrial organisations have launched Connected Frontline Workforce initiatives. **[SECONDARY, vendor blog citing LNS; original report not verified]** — [QAD Redzone blog](https://www.rzsoftware.com/blog/lns-research-connected-workforce-matrix)
- LNS's 2021 connected-workforce spotlight attributes the cross-industry traction to the skills gap "precipitated in large part by workforce demographic shifts". — [LNS Research spotlight](https://lnsresearch.com/research-library/research-articles/research-spotlight-connected-workforce-enable-a-competent-agile-industrial-workforce); a Librestream-sponsored LNS report is at [Librestream PDF](https://cms.librestream.com/media/LibrestreamLNSConnectedWorkerReport.pdf)

### Inferences
- The dominant 2024–2026 pattern is "AI-assisted authoring" (video or documents in, draft SOP out) plus "AI retrieval over work-order history and manuals". Both assume the knowledge already exists somewhere, as a performed task to film or a written record to mine. Neither actively interrogates an expert about judgement calls, cues or exceptions, which leaves a gap for elicitation-first tools.
- Work-order mining (MaintainX and similar) is only as good as the free text technicians wrote. Sparse closure notes, which practitioners widely report as the norm, cap what this can recover. I found no published measurement of this.
- Onboarding/training-time reduction is the de facto outcome metric. Incident reduction and "expert hours spent" are almost never published.

### Gaps
- I found no specific 2024–2026 detail for **Workerbase, Fieldbit, xbird/Ivy, or a product named "Frontline AI copilot"**. Name searches returned nothing relevant.
- I did not fetch Poka/IFS, Tulip or Dozuki primary case studies, so no outcome numbers are recorded for them.
- I found no independent or third-party verification of any vendor outcome figure.
- I did not obtain original LNS or Gartner connected-worker reports; they are paywalled.

## Q2. Expert knowledge-retention programs (nuclear, utilities, oil & gas, aviation): methods, hours per expert, results

### Takeaway
The long-standing playbook has four steps: (1) score knowledge-loss risk (retirement timing × position criticality), (2) prioritise a small set of critical experts, (3) capture through interviews (often videotaped), mentoring, storytelling or communities of practice, and (4) push the captured material into training. Regulators and pioneers repeatedly report that interviews are among the **most expensive** capture methods, and that the unresolved problem is reuse of the captured content, not capture itself. I found **no published hours-per-expert benchmark** in any source.

### Cited Findings
- **TVA (nuclear/utility):** after 10 years of downsizing, TVA faced mass retirement eligibility. From 1999 it surveyed employees annually for planned retirement dates (84% voluntarily gave a non-binding date). It computes **Attrition Factor = Retirement Factor × Position Risk Factor** (the supervisor-rated indispensability of the employee), produces per-site reports, and builds succession plans that include capturing knowledge before departure. It began interviewing senior engineers about critical plant knowledge. — [DeLong, "Better Practices for Retaining Organizational Knowledge", Accenture Institute for Strategic Change, Nov 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf) [PRIMARY/INSTITUTIONAL research report]
- TVA Nuclear formalised the process in 2002. About 35% of its workforce had retired over the previous 6 years, and another 35% would be eligible within 5 years. — [OSTI/ETDE: TVA approach to retaining critical knowledge](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20842949); [OSTI: Knowledge retention and transfer, TVA](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20693032)
- **Delta Air Lines (aviation maintenance, 2001):** 11,000 employees took severance, including 1,200 maintenance technicians and support staff with 20–30 years' experience. Supervisors flagged "critical job loss" cases and interviewed only people who met four criteria: outstanding performer, no backup incumbent, go-to person in crises, and strong internal and external contacts. Technical operations nominated 120 people (66 with more than 40 years' service). The team prioritised by departure date × criticality/uniqueness and **interviewed 85 of the 120 in under a month**, including all of the most critical. — [DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf)
- **Siemens:** if a departing employee's knowledge is judged "crucial and unique", the manager must make extensive retention efforts. The method is chosen by four factors: the expert's willingness and ability, whether they are leaving the company or only moving within it, the timing, and whether a successor is in place. — [DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf)
- **Interview cost and reuse problem:** "interviews, especially when videotaped, are one of the most expensive methods of capturing knowledge… often the only option left when key employees are about to leave." Transcripts "require careful editing, condensing and cataloging to identify 'knowledge nuggets'", and how well interview knowledge gets reapplied "remains unresolved for most organizations". Captured knowledge "does not add value until it is distributed". Only a small share of interviews were videotaped, depending on knowledge type, the interviewee's comfort with the camera and the budget. — [DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf)
- **Metals refinery using lethal chemicals (c. 2000–2001):** the retiring senior superintendent had 27 years on site, and 60% of staff had 25–30 years' experience while most others had about 2. The general manager first planned to hire a journalism student to interview the superintendent. He switched to group sessions with question-based reasoning software (PHRED Solutions) that walked shift teams through: state the problem, analyse impact, temporary fix or root cause, permanent fix. These sessions found the root cause of a tank-liner quality failure that had cost "millions" (glue in liner seams), and the second tank rebuild in 2001 went "without incident". Quote: "I had always assumed people were withholding knowledge, but in reality they just didn't have a forum to share it." Sponsors conceded it was unclear how often the resulting knowledge base would be accessed. — [DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf) [anecdotal, single site]
- **US NRC:** its KM program was formally established in July 2006, driven by a large share of its skilled workforce nearing retirement. It uses departing/retiring-staff interviews, training, databases and procedures. "Knowledge capture interviewing" records staff on video and condenses the result into short clips or transcripts. NRC notes such interviews are often a short-term, end-of-career tactic and says interviewing throughout a career has extra benefits. — [NRC ML14226A962](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1422/ML14226A962.pdf); [NRC ML062060114](https://ww2.nrc.gov/docs/ML0620/ML062060114.pdf); [NRC ML1030 (2010 release)](https://forms.nrc.gov/docs/ML1030/ML103070445.pdf) [PRIMARY; details from search summaries, I could not text-extract the full PDFs]
- **IAEA:** NG-T-6.11 (2017) gives a Knowledge Loss Risk Management methodology focused on employee attrition, with three parts: risk assessment (attrition, position importance, transferability and codifiability of knowledge), mitigation, and effectiveness monitoring. A 2020 IAEA webinar singled out tacit, experience-based knowledge as the hardest to retain and a safety and performance risk. — [IAEA PUB1734 PDF](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/17-35661_PUB1734_web.pdf); [IAEA KLRM page](https://www.iaea.org/node/40917); [INIS record](https://inis.iaea.org/records/gr9qs-knn97) [PRIMARY]
- **Oil & gas "Great Crew Change":** in a 2011 Working Smart/AAPG survey of staff aged 55+, 78% said the crew change was real, with an average planned retirement age of 65 and 23% planning to work beyond it. — [Oil IT Journal 2011](https://oilit.com/HTML_Articles/2011_7_9.php); [Hart Energy](https://www.hartenergy.com/news/great-crew-change-fact-or-fiction-92388). A Houston Chronicle column argues energy companies "forgot the lesson" after the crew change was partly absorbed. — [Houston Chronicle](https://www.houstonchronicle.com/business/columnists/tomlinson/article/Energy-companies-are-forgetting-the-lesson-of-13271735.php). 451 Research frames the crew change as driving IIoT, AR and AI adoption. — [451 Research](https://go.451research.com/2020-mi-great-crew-change-driving-industrial-iot-adoption.html)
- **Deloitte (June 2026) client stories [CONSULTANCY, partly sourced via eGain]:**
  - European gas utility: tenured field engineers were "interviewed intensively" to document diagnostics and safety protocols, which became guided workflows for contact-centre agents. The article's anecdote is internally inconsistent: it says the knowledge "likely saved a life" but also that the home exploded.
  - Automotive manufacturer: a few hundred soon-to-retire SMEs. Pairing knowledge capture with leader alignment, expectations and incentives led to "capture of nearly all mission-critical knowledge". No hours given.
  - European utility: interaction-driven prioritisation built only the highest-priority knowledge, finishing in 1 month instead of the usual 4–5.
  - European telco: unified knowledge base gave first-contact resolution +37%, NPS +30, and time-to-productivity −50%.
  - Statistic: 92% of organisations do not consistently capture knowledge from soon-to-be retirees (APQC via eGain).
  — [Deloitte Insights, "The $9 trillion knowledge exodus"](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/knowledge-management-plan.html)
- **Academic elicitation benchmark (not industry):** a study comparing elicitation techniques on lighting-inspection experts allotted **70 minutes per session**. Structured interviews gave the highest expert agreement on extracted rules (61% acceptance). — [Comparing knowledge elicitation techniques: a case study](https://www.academia.edu/72969336/Comparing_knowledge_elicitation_techniques_a_case_study)
- Practitioner guidance says knowledge transfer for critical roles should start 2–3 years before retirement. — [Glitter.io blog](https://www.glitter.io/blog/knowledge-sharing/knowledge-transfer-retiring-employees) [SECONDARY, low authority]

### Inferences
- The triage step (attrition × criticality, or Delta's four-criteria filter) is the most transferable, evidence-backed element. Programs succeed by capturing a **small, prioritised** set of experts, not everyone.
- Delta's numbers imply a feasible throughput of roughly 85 expert interviews in under 4 weeks with a dedicated team. Per-expert duration was not reported, so hours per expert remains unknown.
- The 20-year-old finding that reuse, not capture, is the bottleneck is the same failure mode modern AI tools claim to solve with retrieval and summarisation. That supports pairing elicitation with retrieval rather than treating them as alternatives.
- The PHRED refinery case suggests that structured, problem-centred group sessions can draw out tacit diagnostic reasoning better than free-form retiree interviews. This is an early analogue of "AI interviewer" elicitation.

### Gaps
- **No source gave hours per expert, cost per interview, or a measured downstream outcome** (incident reduction, time-to-competency) for a retention program. EPRI reports and the full IAEA/NRC PDFs might, but I could not extract them.
- No Duke Energy, BPA, Shell, BP or other named oil & gas operator program with results surfaced. Shell communities of practice are mentioned in DeLong without metrics.
- Most rich case material (TVA, Delta, Siemens) dates from 2001–2005. I found no current (2022–2026) measured program results.

## Q3. AI interview / knowledge products and FM/real-estate-specific offerings (elicitation vs retrieval)

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 market splits into (a) **retrieval** over existing content (Glean-style enterprise search, CMMS copilots, FM-firm AI platforms) and (b) a thin, mostly unproven layer of **elicitation** products (AI-guided interviews or exit interviews) that is largely blog and marketing content with no published outcomes. FM majors (JLL, CBRE) and CMMS vendors (IBM Maximo, Facilio, MaintainX) are firmly in the retrieval and prediction camp. None publicly offers structured expert elicitation.

### Cited Findings
**Elicitation-oriented (AI interviewing / capture)**

It does not clearly describe an AI interviewer. — [Tacit LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/tacitio) [VENDOR, unverified]
- **eGain:** blog on "capturing tacit knowledge from the great retirement cohort using GenAI", with productivity claims. — [eGain blog](https://www.egain.com/blog/capturing-tacit-knowledge-from-the-great-retirement-cohort-using-genai/) [VENDOR]

**Data centres**

- In data centres, the 40% faulty-procedure share suggests expert knowledge capture should target procedure correctness (MOPs, SOPs, EOPs), not just availability. I found no vendor or program capturing data-centre expert know-how specifically.
- ISS and Sodexo: I found no public knowledge-capture program. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

## Q4. Common failure modes reported

### Takeaway
Failures are consistently about **people, process and reuse**, not capture technology. Recurring themes are low frontline adoption when tools add friction, pilots that don't scale, experts lacking time or incentive, captured knowledge that is never reused or never maintained, AI built on fragmented or unvalidated content, and treating KM as an IT or HR project rather than a leadership one.

### Cited Findings
- **Reuse and integration, not capture:** captured knowledge "does not add value until it is distributed". Interview transcripts need heavy editing and cataloguing, and reapplication success "remains unresolved". Sponsors at Quaker Chemical and the metals refinery conceded it was unclear how often their knowledge bases would actually be accessed. "Leading with the technology is a recipe for failure." — [DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf)
- **Bias toward explicit knowledge:** easy-to-document approaches let managers "overlook more important tacit knowledge". — [DeLong/Accenture 2002](https://www.smartworkforcestrategies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DeLong_Better_Practices_for_Retaining_Organizational_Knowledge.pdf); the IAEA likewise flags tacit knowledge as hardest to retain — [IAEA](https://www.iaea.org/node/40917)
- **Expert time and resistance:** job-security fears, fear of change, **lack of time**, perceived threats to expertise. "Daily documentation demands are rarely effective." Tools fail without leadership alignment and incentives. Delegating KM to HR or IT is a failure pattern. Over-documenting creates unsearchable archives. — [Deloitte 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/knowledge-management-plan.html) [CONSULTANCY]
- **AI on fragmented knowledge:** generative AI over fragmented or poorly written knowledge "amplifies gaps" and erodes trust. Automated outputs still need governance and review. — [Deloitte 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/knowledge-management-plan.html); Dozuki's AI drafts still need an approval workflow — [Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/best-dozuki-alternatives-manufacturing/); MaintainX requires human approval of CoPilot output — [MaintainX help](https://help.getmaintainx.com/complete-a-work-order-with-maintainx-copilot)
- **Connected-worker adoption:**
  - Frontline workers bypass tools that require duplicate entry, give generic recommendations or add friction.
  - Pilots succeed with cooperative teams but don't scale across sites and shifts; if every site needs redesign, the pilot has shown only feasibility.
  - Programs track devices deployed rather than outcomes, hiding failure.
  - Integration gaps with CMMS and ERP push work back into spreadsheets.
  — [Smart Industry: pilot to plantwide](https://www.smartindustry.com/benefits-of-transformation/process-innovation/article/55408657/from-pilot-to-plantwide-value-how-to-scale-connected-worker-solutions); [Smart Industry: expectations vs reality](https://www.smartindustry.com/benefits-of-transformation/process-innovation/article/55404081/understanding-connected-worker-solutions-expectations-vs-reality); [Plastics Today](https://www.plasticstoday.com/plastics-processing/smart-factory-evolution-leaves-critical-asset-behind); [TrueContext](https://truecontext.com/blog/connected-worker/) [SECONDARY/VENDOR]
- Gartner (as cited by Smart Industry) warns that adopting technology without building capability (procedure ownership, worker participation, review practices after rollout) creates acquisition risk. — [Smart Industry](https://www.smartindustry.com/benefits-of-transformation/process-innovation/article/55404081/understanding-connected-worker-solutions-expectations-vs-reality)
- A vendor guide cites connected-worker failure rates "as high as 80%" without a primary source. **Treat as unverified.** — [search result set incl. Bonjoy](https://bonjoy.com/articles/what-is-connected-worker-complete-guide-2025/)

- **Procedures themselves are wrong:** 40% of human-error data-centre outages involve faulty procedures. — [Uptime Institute](https://network.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/outages-understanding-human-factor)

### Inferences
- Any elicitation tool needs, by design, (1) low expert time cost and fit into existing work rather than daily documentation demands, (2) a review and governance step, (3) a delivery path into workflows such as work orders or procedures, not a standalone archive, and (4) an owner for keeping content current.
- "Captured knowledge goes stale" is widely implied but rarely documented. No source gave decay rates or update practices.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative data on knowledge staleness or decay, content-maintenance cost, or abandonment rates of captured-knowledge repositories.
- I found no independent survey with measured adoption rates of connected-worker tools after 12+ months. The "80% failure" figure is unsourced.
